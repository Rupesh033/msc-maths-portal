Section A — Short Answer Questions & Solutions

Q1. State Serret-Frenet formulae for space curves. [2 Marks]

Answer: Let \(\vec{t}, \vec{n}, \vec{b}\) be the unit tangent, principal normal, and binormal vectors to a space curve at an arc-length parameter \(s\). The Serret-Frenet formulae are:

\[ \frac{d\vec{t}}{ds} = \kappa \vec{n}, \quad \frac{d\vec{n}}{ds} = -\kappa \vec{t} + \tau \vec{b}, \quad \frac{d\vec{b}}{ds} = -\tau \vec{n} \]

where \(\kappa\) is the curvature and \(\tau\) is the torsion of the curve.

Q2. Define the curvature (\(\kappa\)) and torsion (\(\tau\)) of a space curve. [2 Marks]

Answer:

Curvature (\(\kappa\)): The arc-rate of turning of the unit tangent vector \(\vec{t}\), defined as \(\kappa = \left\|\frac{d\vec{t}}{ds}\right\|\). It measures the deviation of the curve from being a straight line.

Torsion (\(\tau\)): The arc-rate of turning of the binormal vector \(\vec{b}\), defined by \(\frac{d\vec{b}}{ds} = -\tau \vec{n}\). It measures the deviation of the curve from being a plane curve.

Q3. What is the necessary and sufficient condition for a curve to be a plane curve? [2 Marks]

Answer: The necessary and sufficient condition for a curve to be a plane curve is that its torsion \(\tau = 0\) at all points of the curve.

Q4. Define First and Second Fundamental Forms of a surface. [2 Marks]

Answer: For a surface \(\vec{r} = \vec{r}(u, v)\):

First Fundamental Form (\(I\)): \(I = ds^2 = E\,du^2 + 2F\,du\,dv + G\,dv^2\), where \(E = \vec{r}_u \cdot \vec{r}_u\), \(F = \vec{r}_u \cdot \vec{r}_v\), \(G = \vec{r}_v \cdot \vec{r}_v\). It gives the metric on the surface.

Second Fundamental Form (\(II\)): \(II = -d\vec{r} \cdot d\vec{N} = L\,du^2 + 2M\,du\,dv + N\,dv^2\), where \(L = \vec{r}_{uu} \cdot \vec{N}\), \(M = \vec{r}_{uv} \cdot \vec{N}\), \(N = \vec{r}_{vv} \cdot \vec{N}\), and \(\vec{N}\) is the unit normal vector to the surface.

Q5. Define Gaussian curvature \(K\) and Mean curvature \(H\) of a surface. [2 Marks]

Answer: If \(\kappa_1, \kappa_2\) are the principal curvatures of a surface, then:

Gaussian Curvature (\(K\)): \(K = \kappa_1 \kappa_2 = \frac{LN - M^2}{EG - F^2}\).

Mean Curvature (\(H\)): \(H = \frac{1}{2}(\kappa_1 + \kappa_2) = \frac{EN - 2FM + GL}{2(EG - F^2)}\).

Q6. Define contravariant and covariant tensors of rank 1 (vectors). [2 Marks]

Answer: Under a coordinate transformation \(x^i \to \bar{x}^i\):

A set of quantities \(A^i\) constitutes a contravariant vector (rank 1) if it transforms as: \[ \bar{A}^i = \frac{\partial \bar{x}^i}{\partial x^j} A^j \]

A set of quantities \(A_i\) constitutes a covariant vector (rank 1) if it transforms as: \[ \bar{A}_i = \frac{\partial x^j}{\partial \bar{x}^i} A_j \]

Q7. Define Christoffel symbols of the first and second kinds. [2 Marks]

Answer: In terms of the metric tensor \(g_{ij}\):

First Kind: \([ij, k] = \Gamma_{ijk} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial g_{ik}}{\partial x^j} + \frac{\partial g_{jk}}{\partial x^i} - \frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial x^k}\right)\)

Second Kind: \(\left\{ \begin{matrix} k \\ ij \end{matrix} \right\} = \Gamma^k_{ij} = g^{km} [ij, m]\)

Q8. State Bianchi Identities for the Riemann-Christoffel curvature tensor. [2 Marks]

Answer: The Bianchi identity states that the covariant derivative of the curvature tensor satisfies:

\[ R^l_{ijk, m} + R^l_{ikm, j} + R^l_{imj, k} = 0 \]

where the comma denotes covariant differentiation with respect to the index.
